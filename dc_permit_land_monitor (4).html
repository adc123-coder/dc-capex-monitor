Monthly Commentary — June 2026
1
Texas has structurally displaced Northern Virginia as the world's primary data center market: Texas overtook NOVA for the first time in June 2026, driven by faster permitting, no moratorium risk, utility cooperation, and a political environment actively welcoming large-load development. CloudBurst broke ground on a 1.2 GW flagship campus, Nvidia partnered with IREN for up to 5 GW at Sweetwater, and Vantage secured permits for 1,644 acres in Wisconsin. This geographic rotation is now visible in both the land and starts signals — the composite's headline numbers are increasingly Texas-driven, masking continued deterioration in legacy markets.
2
Construction starts rebounding to 36/mo (+24% MoM) — but the recovery is geographically narrow: The June jump is almost entirely attributable to two large Texas groundbreakings (CloudBurst 1.2 GW, Nvidia/IREN Sweetwater). Strip Texas and starts are still running well below the March 2026 peak of 38. The structural headwind — a thin approval cohort from Oct '25–Mar '26 — has not cleared. Expect starts outside Texas to remain suppressed through Q3 2026.
3
Approval rate at 51% — new cycle-low — with New York's legislature passing a moratorium on June 4: New York's legislature passed a one-year moratorium on data centers of 20 MW or more, pending the Governor's signature. At least 14 states now have active or enacted moratorium measures. Virginia is tightening further: revised permitting guidance from July 1 challenges the assumption that hyperscale backup generators are "rarely used," adding air permit complexity and cost to every new NOVA filing. Oklahoma's new ratepayer protection act — requiring large-load AI projects to fund their own infrastructure costs — is a new financial gate model likely to spread to other states.
4
The composite index is held above zero by land and velocity — both increasingly Texas-driven: Land acquisition (5,650 acres MTD, +5% MoM) and permit velocity (265 MTD, +6% MoM) are the composite's two highest-weighted signals at 30% each, and both are positive. But the approval rate at 51% and friction at 14 blocks are at historic lows and highs respectively. The index is telling a two-speed story: the demand pipeline is intact and accelerating in permissive markets; the regulatory environment is deteriorating in legacy and emerging markets simultaneously. The PJM NJ freeze expired in early June — a modest positive — but the New York moratorium more than offsets it.
5
Cost-of-infrastructure model shifting nationally — the Wisconsin and Oklahoma precedents are the signal to watch: Wisconsin's We Energies now requires large-load customers to fund the full cost of new generation and grid infrastructure. Oklahoma's Data Center Consumer Ratepayer Protection Act codifies the same principle at state level. North Carolina is advancing similar legislation. If this model spreads to Virginia, Texas, or Ohio, it would materially increase the financial barrier to groundbreaking and introduce a new leading indicator of capex stress not currently captured in this index.
Regulatory Blocks & Moratoria — Active Jul 3, 2026
New York — State Moratorium Pending Governor
⚑ NEW · Jun 4
NY legislature passed a 1-year moratorium on data centers ≥20 MW, pending Governor Hochul's signature. Would affect the NJ/NYC market bloc directly. At least 14 states now have proposed or enacted similar measures. Governor decision expected in July.
Virginia — Tightened Generator Permitting (Jul 1)
⚑ NEW · Jul 1
Revised guidance effective July 1 challenges the assumption that hyperscale backup generators are "rarely used," requiring air permits at a higher threshold. Increases cost and timeline for every new NOVA data center filing. Applies to all applications submitted on or after July 1, 2026.
N. Virginia — Dominion Energy Interconnection Hold
Ongoing · Since Mar 14
Hold on new large-load interconnection requests (>10 MW) in Loudoun & Prince William counties. ~18 permits / ~2,400 MW affected. Capacity study ongoing — resolution expected Q3 2026.
N. Virginia — Loudoun County Moratorium
Review · Jun 2026
Original moratorium ran through Jun 2026 (third extension). Board of Supervisors reviewing extension vs. a permanent regulatory framework. Outcome expected July 2026. A fourth extension or permanent rule would maintain the block; a framework resolution would remove it.
Minneapolis, MN — 6-Month Moratorium
Ongoing · Since May 22
6-month moratorium on data centers >350,000 sq ft, running through ~Nov 2026. Minnesota statewide bill still in session — if enacted, would freeze permitting until mid-2028.
Hill County, TX — 1-Year Moratorium (Lawsuit Pending)
Ongoing · Since May 12
Developer with 1,235 MW Project Aquila is suing. $61.75M PJM deposit due Jul 24, 2026. Court outcome in July is the key near-term watch item for the DFW corridor.
Denver, CO — City Data Center Moratorium
Ongoing · Since Apr '26
Moratorium on new data center approvals ongoing while Denver reassesses regulations. Expected through Q3 2026.
New Jersey — PJM Interconnection Freeze
EXPIRED · Early Jun 2026
90-day freeze expired early June 2026 without renewal. NJ/NYC load zone interconnection applications are again open. Modestly positive for the panel — partially offset by the New York moratorium.
Phoenix — ADWR Water Adequacy Review
Watching · Since Feb 28
2 large-campus permits (>500 acres) under enhanced water review. 60–90 day delay. Not yet a formal moratorium.
Chicago — ComEd Transmission Upgrade Delay
Ongoing · Since Nov '25
Manteno–Chicago North 345kV upgrade delayed to Q4 2026. ~280 MW on hold.
