Why IQ Leads
Every datacenter must secure grid access through an ISO interconnection queue — a process that takes 2–5 years, making queue dynamics the earliest observable proxy for DC capex intentions. Filing a grid-connection request commits developer resources 2–4 years before a shovel touches the ground. Historically, queue reversals have preceded visible capex cuts by 12–18 months; given the unprecedented depth of the current backlog, that transmission lag could extend to 18–30 months. The 60% weight on net MW momentum is the dominant signal: when additions no longer exceed withdrawals, the committed pipeline is actively contracting. Withdrawal acceleration (20%) provides the earliest warning — outflow volumes tripled before net MW turned negative. Wait-time elongation (20%) captures the structural deterrent to new entrants that does not mean-revert without ISO reform.

Monthly Commentary — June 2026
1
FERC's June 18 show cause orders are the dominant event — 100% cost assignment to large loads triggers a withdrawal surge: FERC issued six orders to all jurisdictional RTOs requiring them to justify or revise their large load interconnection rules, with a clear preference for assigning 100% of network upgrade costs to the interconnecting load. Projects relying on socialised upgrade costs — a meaningful share of the queue — now face binary decisions: pay or exit. June withdrawals jumped to 4,610 MW (+260 MW MoM), the sharpest acceleration since Nov '25. Composite: ….
2
Net MW +70 MW — the thinnest positive reading in the 27-month window: Additions bounced to 4,680 MW as some developers raced to file before FERC cost rules are formalised, but the simultaneous withdrawal surge nearly cancelled the effect. The near-wash leaves net MW barely above zero. Jun '25 saw 9,278 MW net positive; Jun '26 is +70 MW — a 99% year-on-year collapse in net flow. The 3-month net (Apr–Jun) is +270 MW, technically positive but hollow — it reflects April's PJM intake bounce, not underlying health.
3
PJM's Expedited Interconnection Track (EIT) targeting July 31, 2026 effective date — a potential partial offset: PJM's EIT framework creates an expedited 10-month study process for up to 10 generator interconnection requests per year paired with datacenter loads (Bring Your Own Generation). If approved and adopted quickly, this could stimulate a new wave of paired generation + load filings in H2 2026. This is a watch item: it would not be visible in the queue data until Q4 at the earliest, and it only benefits projects able to pair with firm baseload or storage.
4
Wait time hit 4.6 years — P90 at 7.5 years, approaching the 5-year median flagged by 2026 industry data: The avg-to-P90 gap (4.6yr vs 7.5yr) has been widening steadily since mid-2025, reflecting ongoing bifurcation: straightforward low-upgrade projects proceed; capital-intensive projects with congestion exposure face indefinite delays. Google has publicly cited potential grid connection delays of up to 12 years for new datacenters in congested ISOs. RTOs now have 60–90 days to respond to FERC's show cause orders; actual process changes will not register in wait time metrics before H2 2027.
5
ERCOT speculative filing flush accelerating — Texas SB6 filters taking effect: As of April 2026, ERCOT had 410 GW of large-load requests under assessment, 87% from datacenters — nearly five times the grid's 85 GW peak demand. Texas SB6 (signed Jul '25) introduced real filters: mandatory $100k study fee, site control documentation, curtailment terms. Speculative and phantom filings are now exiting. This is the right structural correction but mechanically adds to withdrawal counts and weighs on the net MW signal in the near term without representing demand destruction.
